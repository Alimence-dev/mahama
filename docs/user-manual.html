Section 1

Overview

The system tracks resources — food, hygiene items, and clothing/materials — from request through to the moment a family actually receives them, closing the gap between "approved" and "distributed."

Five roles appear in the system. Four of them log in; the fifth does not:

RoleWhat they do
AdminRegisters user accounts, restocks inventory, views reports.
VolunteerRegisters families, manages inventory, submits requests, distributes resources.
ManagerReviews requests and forwards them to a donor, or rejects them. Views family records.
DonorApproves or rejects requests forwarded by a manager.
RefugeesNot a login role. Families are data records that volunteers register and act on — see below.

The family unit

Every person registered is part of a family — a single person is simply a family of size one. Requests, approvals, and distributions are always tied to the family, never to one individual, so a household of five is served as one unit.

The request lifecycle

A resource request moves through a fixed sequence, and every step is logged:

StatusSet byMeaning
pendingVolunteerRequest submitted, awaiting the manager's review.
manager_approvedManagerForwarded to a donor for approval.
donor_approvedDonorApproved — ready for the volunteer to hand out.
rejectedManager or DonorDeclined at either review step.
distributedVolunteerPhysically handed to the family. Stock is deducted and a notification is sent at this step, not before.

Section 2

Getting started

Open the application in a browser. You'll land on the introduction page:

Click Log in and sign in with the email and password your Admin issued you:

Sessions do not survive a page reload. This is deliberate: the app keeps you signed in only in memory, not in browser storage. Refreshing the page or closing the tab logs you out — this is expected, not a bug. If you're timed out mid-session (after 8 hours), you'll be returned to the login screen with a message explaining why.

Admin

Admin guide

Admins control who has access to the system and keep the camp's stock topped up.

Registering a new user

  1. Fill in Name, Email, and a temporary Password in the "Register new user" form.
  2. Choose their Role — admin, volunteer, manager, or donor.
  3. Click Create user. They can log in immediately with the credentials you set.

Only Admins can create accounts — there is no public sign-up.

Restocking inventory

When a shipment arrives, find the item in the "Inventory restock" table, enter the quantity received, and click Restock. This is the only way stock increases — every restock is timestamped and attributed to the admin who logged it, so there's always an audit trail from zero to whatever is on the shelf.

Items showing Low stock next to their quantity have fallen below the threshold set when the item was created.

Volunteer

Volunteer guide

Volunteers do the most hands-on work: registering families, keeping inventory current, submitting requests on a family's behalf, and physically handing out resources.

Registering a family

Click + Register new family. Every registration uses the same form, whether it's one person or ten:

  1. The first entry is always the head of family (relationship self) — fill in name, age, gender, and an optional phone number.
  2. For a single person, stop there and click Register family.
  3. For a household, click Add family member for each additional person, and choose how they relate to the head — spouse, child, parent, or sibling.
  4. If someone other than the head should be allowed to collect resources when the head isn't available, mark them as the secondary contact using the radio button next to their entry.
Phone numbers Enter numbers as either local format (07XXXXXXXX) or international (+250XXXXXXXXX) — the field converts local numbers to the international format automatically when you tab out of it. A family with no phone on file is not a problem: distribution falls back to a printed voucher instead of SMS.

Editing a family

Open a family from the dashboard list to add members later, edit a member's details, or change the secondary contact:

Managing inventory and submitting requests

The Inventory & requests page is where day-to-day stock and requests happen:

  • Add inventory item — register a new item under Food, Hygiene, or Clothing & Materials, with a starting quantity and a low-stock threshold.
  • Submit resource request — pick the family, the item, and a quantity. The system won't let you request more than is currently in stock.

Distributing to a family

Once a request reaches donor_approved, it appears on the Distribution page:

  1. Choose who is collecting — the head of family or the secondary contact, if one was set.
  2. Click Mark distributed.
  3. The system deducts the quantity from stock, logs the handover with a timestamp, and sends a notification — by SMS if the family has a phone on file, or a printable voucher if not. The result appears immediately, with a View voucher button when applicable.

Manager

Manager guide

Managers are the first checkpoint on every request, and can look up any family's record for context.

Reviewing requests

  1. Every new request appears under Pending requests, showing the family, item, quantity, and who requested it.
  2. Click Forward to donor to send it on for donor approval, or Reject to decline it outright.
  3. Request history below shows every request this camp has ever made, with its current status.

Looking up a family

Click Families in the navigation to browse every registered family and drill into a household's members — read-only, since editing family records is a Volunteer responsibility.

Donor

Donor guide

Donors give the final sign-off before a family can receive anything.

  1. Requests a manager has forwarded appear under Requests forwarded by manager.
  2. Click Approve to clear it for distribution, or Reject to decline it.
  3. Current inventory levels are shown above, for context on what's realistic to fund.

Available to Admin & Manager

Reports

A shared reporting page for oversight — a live count of requests by status, and the complete distribution history, each exportable as CSV.

Section 8

Notifications explained

Every distribution triggers exactly one notification attempt, chosen automatically:

ConditionChannelWhat happens
Family head has a phone number on file SMS A real request is sent to the Africa's Talking SMS gateway (sandbox). Whatever comes back — success or failure — is stored as-is; nothing is faked.
No phone number on file Voucher A printable HTML voucher is generated instead, showing the family, item, quantity, and timestamp — proof of distribution with no dependency on owning a phone.

Because this is a genuine API integration rather than a simulated one, an SMS attempt can fail for real-world reasons — invalid sandbox credentials, or a malformed phone number. That failure is recorded transparently in the notification's status rather than hidden, which is deliberate: the distribution itself still succeeds and is logged, even if the SMS does not go through.

Section 9

Troubleshooting & FAQ

I got logged out for no reason.

Either you reloaded the page (sessions are memory-only by design — see Getting started), or 8 hours passed since you logged in. Log in again to continue.

Why won't it let me submit a request for this quantity?

The system blocks any request larger than what's currently in stock, so the camp never promises more than it has. Check the inventory table for the current available quantity.

A distribution's SMS shows "failed" — did the handover fail too?

No. Distribution and notification are separate steps: stock is deducted and the handover is logged regardless of whether the SMS succeeds. A failed SMS just means the family wasn't reached electronically — reprint via the voucher path if needed, or follow up in person.

Can a refugee log in to check their own status?

No — this is intentional. Refugees are records managed by Volunteers, not system users. See Overview.

I need to correct a family member's phone number or details.

A Volunteer can edit any member from the family's detail page (Edit next to their row) — Managers and Donors have read-only or no access to family records.
